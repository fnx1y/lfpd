Guidelines

The Los Francisco Police Department is a community first. Whether you are taking part in a training, playing on the server, talking in Discord, or simply meeting other members, the way you treat people matters.

These guidelines exist to help keep LFPD an enjoyable place for everyone. They are not intended to make every interaction feel overly formal. Instead, they provide a general standard for how members are expected to conduct themselves while participating in the community.

Treat people with respect

Everyone in the community is here for a different reason. Some people are here to roleplay, some are here to train, and others simply enjoy being part of the community.

Treat other members, instructors, staff, and visitors with basic respect. Disagreements happen, but personal attacks, harassment, targeted behaviour, and deliberately trying to make someone else's experience worse are not acceptable.

Community conduct

Use common sense when communicating with others. Keep conversations appropriate for the space you are in and avoid behaviour that is deliberately disruptive.

  • Do not harass or target other members.
  • Do not deliberately disrupt trainings, sessions, or department activities.
  • Do not impersonate members of the department or other community members.
  • Do not intentionally create unnecessary conflict.
  • Follow the instructions of staff and event hosts when participating in official activities.

Roleplay and department activities

Roleplay is at the heart of what the LFPD community does. That means making an effort to contribute to the experience rather than intentionally ruining it for everyone else.

During official activities, members are expected to follow the instructions of the person leading the session. If something goes wrong or you believe an issue needs attention, report it through the appropriate channels rather than turning the situation into an argument.

Moderation and enforcement

Staff may take action when behaviour violates these guidelines or negatively affects the community. The response may depend on the situation, its seriousness, and whether similar behaviour has happened before.

If you believe a moderation decision was made incorrectly, use the appropriate reporting or appeal process instead of attempting to escalate the situation publicly.

The simple version

Be respectful. Use common sense. Let other people enjoy the community too.

That is really what these guidelines come down to.
